OCTOBER CURRENTS — LAKE ONTARIO & THE ST. LAWRENCE

Suggested forum caption

A map of mean modelled water motion from Lake Ontario into the St. Lawrence, with a separately labelled experimental Lake Simcoe wind response and a Montréal detail. The fields represent 12 October 2026 at 00:00 UTC through 17 October at 12:00 UTC. The retrieved government products use 23 equally weighted samples, six hours apart; Simcoe uses a time-integrated numerical experiment over the same period. Animated particles follow these fixed average fields, with motion accelerated for visibility; they do not replay changing conditions through the week.

Lake Ontario uses NOAA surface-layer nowcasts. The Montréal and fluvial-river sections use ECCC depth-averaged analyses, while the downstream estuary uses archived DFO surface forecasts for those same valid times. The average preserves direction, so opposing flows can cancel; it is not the average speed of moving water.

The Simcoe layer is an unvalidated depth-averaged wind response computed from public bathymetry and observed Barrie-Oro winds. It excludes thermal circulation, surface shear and inflow/outlet exchange. It is not a measured or government current field. The Simcoe close-up has its own labelled linear 0–5 cm/s colour scale; the overview uses the logarithmic scale.

The geography includes the previously missing upper river and water around both sides of Montréal Island. Retrieved velocity products still leave gaps in parts of the upper river, Montréal’s northern channels and the outer estuary/Gulf. Those waters remain visible. This is not complete current coverage of the river or a validated estimate of Simcoe's actual circulation.

SOURCE AND METHODS NOTES

1. Fixed period and meaning of the average

Start: 2026-10-12T00:00:00Z.
End:   2026-10-17T12:00:00Z.
Government product samples: 23, spaced six hours apart.
Times: 12–16 October at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC; 17 October at 00:00, 06:00 and 12:00 UTC.

This is a mean for the current week to date, using 2026 model fields. It is not an October climatology, a complete seven-day average, or a set of direct current measurements. Every displayed retained government-product velocity cell has all 23 valid samples. Those samples receive equal weight, including the endpoints. The separate Simcoe experiment integrates u/v at every 10-second solver step; its 23 six-hour snapshots are retained for inspection but are not the averaging weights.

Eastward and northward components are averaged separately:
  mean_u = sum(u) / 23
  mean_v = sum(v) / 23
  magnitude of the mean vector = sqrt(mean_u² + mean_v²)

That magnitude differs from mean speed:
  mean_speed = sum(sqrt(u² + v²)) / 23

Reversing tidal or wind-driven currents can yield a small mean vector even when water moved rapidly at individual times. Averaging speed alone would discard this cancellation and would not supply a meaningful mean direction. This export uses the vector mean. Any animated particle motion follows the resulting stationary field and is a visual trace, not the reconstructed path of a water parcel during the sampled period.

2. Lake Ontario — NOAA LOOFS

Product: Lake Ontario Operational Forecast System, native unstructured model mesh, top sigma layer (layer index 0).
Inputs: 23 completed nowcast fields, one at each selected valid time. The n006 field gives the cycle’s ending nowcast time; it is not a six-hour forecast in this selection.
Velocity units: metres per second; temperature units: degrees Celsius.
Native dimensions: 64,453 vector faces and 34,395 temperature nodes. Surface-layer thickness is model-dependent; it is not a fixed one-metre observation depth.
Temperature, where used, is averaged separately at mesh nodes. It does not supply temperatures for Simcoe or the Canadian river products.

3. Montréal and fluvial river — ECCC OHPS

Product: Operational Hydrodynamic Prediction System, St. Lawrence River and fluvial estuary, 100-metre native polar-stereographic grid.
Inputs: 23 zero-hour model analyses at the selected valid times, obtained from ECCC’s retained archive.
Layer: depth-averaged velocity. This is not directly equivalent to the surface-layer LOOFS or surface STLE200 field.

Native projection x/y components are rotated into geographic east/north using local projection convergence. The projection uses central longitude −433° (equivalent to −73°), pole scale 0.9330127, and spherical radius 6,370,997 m. Rounded projection offsets were anchored to the supplied native longitude/latitude at cell (0,0); remote corner and centre checks agree within 5 metres. Display grids use the nearest native cell, without extending currents into missing regions.

Two display resolutions were prepared from the same fields and period: a 1200 × 900 river overview and a 1000 × 701 Montréal detail. Their respective valid-vector counts are 36,873 and 50,170. All retained cells have 23 valid samples; no erosion of the velocity mask was added.

STLE component-orientation audit

The distributed NetCDF variables are called uos/vos and have generic sea-water x/y velocity labels. Their supplied coordinates form a rotated curvilinear grid. Those labels alone do not conclusively document whether distribution preprocessing already converted the components into geographic east/north.

An additional native-grid rotation was tested and rejected. Across 11,489 faster cells in narrow channels, treating the supplied components as east/north aligned with the local wet-channel principal axis with mean absolute dot product 0.98195 and median 0.99442. Applying a further approximately 37.8° grid rotation reduced these to 0.77789 and 0.79014, producing cross-channel directions in places such as Trois-Rivières. The export therefore retains the distributed components as supplied and interprets them as east/north.

This is an empirical orientation check against channel geometry, not documentary confirmation of the distributor’s preprocessing, nor validation against observed currents. The remaining metadata ambiguity is recorded here rather than presented as conclusively resolved by the source labels.

The geographic display grid is 1400 × 1100, with 66,334 valid cells drawn from 112,532 native wet cells. All retained native and display cells have 23 valid samples. Nearest-cell resampling includes native land nodes, preserving the available wet/dry mask instead of drawing currents across land.

5. Geographic gaps and Lake Simcoe

The export shows continuous sourced water geography, including Lake Simcoe, the Thousand Islands, the upper river through Brockville and Cornwall, and channels around Montréal Island and Laval. Complete geography does not imply complete velocity coverage.

The retrieved model products leave velocity gaps along parts of the upper river between the Lake Ontario model and the Montréal/fluvial model, including the Brockville–Cornwall corridor. Montréal’s northern channels also lack a retrieved gridded velocity field usable for the selected dates. No added current field fills those river gaps.

Lake Simcoe has a separately computed experimental, unvalidated depth-averaged wind response. It solves linearized rotating shallow-water equations on a 500 m Arakawa C grid, using historical Ontario depth contours, the mapped shoreline/islands and 408 consecutive ECCC Barrie-Oro hourly wind observations. Wind speed is converted from km/h to m/s, and meteorological direction from (tens of degrees true) is converted to east/north components. The station wind is applied uniformly without an over-water adjustment; nominal 10 m height is assumed rather than verified for this specific instrument.

The preferred run begins at rest on 1 October 2026 at 05:00 UTC, uses 259 hours of wind forcing before the averaging window, and integrates mean u/v over 12 October 00:00–17 October 12:00 UTC. Pressure gradients, Coriolis, wind stress and quadratic bottom drag are included. Atherley Narrows and wet/dry faces are closed; no inflows, outflows, thermal gradients, internal waves or vertical shear are included. Mean-vector maximum is approximately 3.80 cm/s and median 0.735 cm/s for this experiment. These values are not validated observations.

Numerical rest, mass, energy-budget, drag, boundary, direction and timestep tests passed. Shorter forcing history, altered drag and coarser grid change the result materially; these are sensitivity experiments, not confidence intervals. Actual Lake Simcoe circulation remains unresolved. 7. Rendering and media

The map is rendered from model vectors and sourced geographic geometry with native Canvas and FFmpeg. It contains no AI-generated map imagery, website controls or timeline. The overview, Montréal detail and enlarged Simcoe close-up animate their fixed vector means.

The speed legend is logarithmic, with ticks at 0, 2, 10, 50 and 150+ cm/s. Colour represents the magnitude of the mean velocity vector. Speeds at or above 1.5 m/s share the top colour. The larger Simcoe close-up uses a separate linear 0–5 cm/s palette with brighter low-speed colours and thicker, longer-persisting trails so the flow remains visible when viewing the entire composition. Its numerical vectors are unchanged. Particle motion is accelerated by a nominal factor of 90,000 in the overview and Montréal detail, and 180,000 in the enlarged Simcoe detail; particle lifetime, shoreline termination and the final loop transition are display choices, so these are illustrative flow traces rather than Lagrangian particle simulations.

The exported video is a silent 20-second H.264 loop, 2560 × 1920 pixels at 30 frames per second, with a two-second crossfade that joins the loop. Rendering is deterministic for the chosen seed.


8. Broader outer estuary and drainage revision

The formerly blank outer estuary is supplemented by archived DFO POPS STLE500 surface velocities for the same 23 six-hour samples, 12 October 2026 00:00 UTC through 17 October 12:00 UTC. Both components are averaged separately. The broader product is fill-only: existing NOAA, OHPS, STLE200 and separately identified Simcoe cells retain their vectors. All 89,900 valid full display cells contain all 23 samples. The new field reaches both northeast artwork edges. Coastal cells missing from both products remain absent; no velocity is extrapolated into them.

The full STLE500 model domain is also included on the website, retaining this fixed archived mean and explicitly showing its period separately from the sources that refresh. It does not become a current forecast or a 24-hour mean when those website modes change. See docs/outer-estuary.md for sources, byte-record hashes, component-orientation checks and resampling details.

The main film map uses a brighter low-speed palette, thicker visible trails and a matching logarithmic 0–150+ cm/s legend. These display changes do not alter the current vectors. Narrow fluvial model cells receive additional particle seeding for legibility at the whole-map scale. Particle density is a display choice and does not encode discharge, mass flux or confidence. The enlarged Simcoe inset retains its own linear 0–5 cm/s legend. Motion remains accelerated; the film is a fixed mean, not changing weather replay.

The static drainage overlay uses the official LSRCA scientific Simcoe watershed and 4,050 stream-order-three-or-higher source watercourse segments, grouped into 82 named systems. The gold dashed line is the actual shared boundary of Ontario's Lake Huron and Lake Ontario basin polygons, generalized for this map. Locally near the Oak Ridges Moraine, the northern Simcoe side drains through Lake Couchiching and the Severn to Georgian Bay (Lake Huron), while the southern side drains to Lake Ontario. Farther east, northern moraine catchments can drain via the Trent system to Lake Ontario. The line is not a traced moraine conservation-plan extent. Watercourse vertex order is not asserted to be downstream, and no current speeds or discharge are invented for these tributaries.

10. Original Kingston–Brockville estimates edition, 19 October 2026 (superseded below)

The featured 20-second Lake Ontario film now adds a new, explicitly estimated river field between the Kingston/Cape Vincent boundary and Brockville. The lake's original 23-snapshot surface-vector mean is unchanged. The river is a steady, simplified depth-averaged diffusion-wave hydraulic scenario forced with the same period's mean endpoint stages and calibrated to the IJC regulation-discharge target. It is not a temporal average of hourly river velocities, measured currents, or an operational forecast. The film footer reads: "Kingston–Brockville: ESTIMATES · simplified, unvalidated depth-averaged model".

The 50 m finite-volume calculation uses CHS NONNA numerical bathymetry and sourced water polygons preserving island holes. About 82.04% of the connected input corridor has source-depth support; the remaining 17.96% is explicitly estimated bank bed. Historical survey datums are represented by an approximate chart-datum separation surface, not a verified modern vertical transformation. All source masks, frozen inputs, method code and audit results are published under model/upper-river/.

This approximation conserves the prescribed scenario discharge; that is a numerical/calibration property, not validation of local velocities. A held-out Alexandria Bay water-level check differs by about +2.4 cm, and 50/100 m results have broadly similar main-flow directions. Neither establishes current accuracy. Inertia, inertial eddies, wind, stratification and surface shear are omitted. An experimental full-momentum comparison was unsettled and materially different; its failure and kinetic-head defects in a small set of fast cells are documented. Local jet speeds should not be read as quantitatively reconstructed observations.

The river estimate only fills film cells without NOAA data. Its extra particle seeding improves legibility and does not represent greater discharge or confidence. No vectors are stretched past the Brockville outlet. The broader live atlas retains its previous domains and coverage; this fixed river estimate is not silently added to its live sources. Detailed assumptions, sensitivities, reproducible commands, source URLs and limitations: docs/kingston-brockville-estimates.md and model/upper-river/README.md.
